Q1 2025: +31.4%
A Full Account

Twenty-two trades. Sixteen winners. Two that should have been avoided entirely. One that was cut far too early. Here is the honest, complete picture of the strongest quarter on record — and exactly what it means going into Q2.

The context

January opened with gold sitting at $2,620 having recovered from the December pullback. The macro backdrop was constructive — DXY showing signs of exhaustion, US 10-year yields topping out, and geopolitical tension providing a consistent bid beneath the market. The directional bias was long from the first week of the year.

This is not to say the quarter was easy. The February fakeout at $2,940 was a real test of process. Price ran stops above the prior high by twelve pips before reversing aggressively. The loss was taken cleanly. There was no hesitation and no attempt to re-enter in the same session.

Discipline is not what you do when the trade works. It is what you do when it does not.

The trades that worked

The 14 January demand block long at $2,640 was the clearest setup of the quarter. Three confluences aligned: an untested weekly demand block, a fair value gap from the 4H chart, and a fresh break of structure on the 1H that confirmed institutional participation. The target was the weekly supply at $2,740. The trade ran 3.2R with the runner closed at $2,758.

The 5 March break of structure entry was the highest conviction trade of the quarter. Price had been ranging between $2,900 and $2,960 for eleven sessions. When the break came, it came on the highest volume candle of the month. The position was sized at 1.5× standard risk in recognition of the setup quality. It closed at 3.8R — the best single trade since Q3 2023.

Note on position sizing: All trades use a fixed fractional risk model — 1% of account per standard lot, scaled by setup quality (0.5×, 1×, or 1.5×). No trade ever risks more than 1.5% of the account. This has not changed since the model was adopted in Q2 2022.

The trades that did not

The 8 February short at $2,940 was stopped out. In retrospect, the supply zone was invalidated by the strength of the preceding move — price had arrived at that level with too much momentum to respect it cleanly. The setup met the criteria on paper, but the context was wrong. This has been added to the criteria checklist for supply shorts: momentum of approach must be evaluated, not just the zone itself.

The 21 March short was a different lesson entirely. The setup was valid. The zone held. But the stop was placed too tightly — six pips above the high of the zone rather than the standard ten. Price swept the stop by four pips and then fell 180 pips without me. This one stings. The lesson is not to second-guess the model's stop placement methodology.

What changes in Q2

Two adjustments take effect from April. First, supply shorts must pass a momentum filter — if the preceding bullish leg is greater than 1.5× the average true range of the previous twenty sessions, the short will not be taken regardless of zone quality. Second, stop placement reverts strictly to the model default. No discretionary tightening.

The position sizing adjustment made in Q3 2022 — scaling to 1.5× on the highest quality setups — will continue. The Q1 evidence supports it: the three 1.5× trades achieved an average of 3.1R, compared to 1.8R for the standard-sized trades.

The record

All twenty-two trades from Q1 2025 are documented in the performance section of this site with full entry and exit data. The TradingView published ideas for each setup are linked from the trade log. Nothing has been edited after the fact. The timestamps are permanent.
